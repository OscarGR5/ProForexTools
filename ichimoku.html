Ichimoku Kinko Hyo

What is Ichimoku Kinko Hyo?

Ichimoku Kinko Hyo, often referred to as Ichimoku, is a comprehensive indicator system used in technical analysis. It was developed by Goichi Hosoda, a Japanese journalist, and published in his 1969 book. The term "Ichimoku Kinko Hyo" translates to "one glance equilibrium chart," indicating its ability to provide a snapshot of the market's equilibrium state.

Components of Ichimoku

Ichimoku consists of five main components:

  • Tenkan-sen (Conversion Line)
  • Kijun-sen (Base Line)
  • Senkou Span A (Leading Span A)
  • Senkou Span B (Leading Span B)
  • Chikou Span (Lagging Span)

Tenkan-sen (Conversion Line)

The Tenkan-sen is calculated as the average of the highest high and the lowest low over the past 9 periods.

Formula: (Highest High + Lowest Low) / 2 over the last 9 periods

Kijun-sen (Base Line)

The Kijun-sen is calculated as the average of the highest high and the lowest low over the past 26 periods.

Formula: (Highest High + Lowest Low) / 2 over the last 26 periods

Senkou Span A (Leading Span A)

Senkou Span A is the average of the Tenkan-sen and Kijun-sen, plotted 26 periods ahead.

Formula: (Tenkan-sen + Kijun-sen) / 2, plotted 26 periods ahead

Senkou Span B (Leading Span B)

Senkou Span B is the average of the highest high and the lowest low over the past 52 periods, plotted 26 periods ahead.

Formula: (Highest High + Lowest Low) / 2 over the last 52 periods, plotted 26 periods ahead

Kumo (Cloud)

The area between Senkou Span A and Senkou Span B is called the Kumo, or cloud. The Kumo is a key feature of the Ichimoku system, providing dynamic support and resistance levels.

Chikou Span (Lagging Span)

The Chikou Span is the current closing price plotted 26 periods back. It is used to gauge market sentiment.

Formula: Current closing price, plotted 26 periods back

How Ichimoku Works

Ichimoku Kinko Hyo is designed to provide a comprehensive view of market conditions. By combining trend, momentum, and support/resistance elements, Ichimoku helps traders make informed decisions. Here's how it works:

  • When the price is above the Kumo, the market is considered bullish.
  • When the price is below the Kumo, the market is considered bearish.
  • The Tenkan-sen and Kijun-sen crossovers provide trading signals.
  • The Chikou Span confirms the trend direction.
  • The Kumo's thickness indicates the strength of the support/resistance.
